Distraction blockers

Put a wall between you and the sites and apps that swallow your afternoon - stronger than willpower, and free to try.

Cold Turkey Blocker (free version)

Start hereFreeware

Blocks whole sites and apps in one click, and lets itself be trusted: the free version never asks you to pay for the core job.

Add the sites and applications that trap you to a block list, turn blocking on, and nothing gets through until you say so. The free tier blocks unlimited sites and apps for as long as you set it, and unlike a timer app it also stops the desktop apps - the games, the email client, the chat window.

Start hereOn Windows this is the strongest free block you can put between you and a bad habit - one lazy afternoon of setup buys weeks of quieter evenings if it clicks.

  • Why it is freeThe free version is a full blocker, not a trial: unlimited blocks that last as long as you lock them for, with no expiry date and no nagging pop-ups. Schedules, usage reports, and blocking-resistance upgrades are clearly separated as paid extras on the official site; the core block job is free forever.
  • Works onWindows PC, Mac
  • Honest limitsWindows and Mac; the free tier cannot pause active blocks or use schedules.

Screen Time (iPhone and iPad)

Start hereBuilt in

The blocker already inside your iPhone: daily app limits, scheduled downtime, and a passcode to protect you from yourself.

Open Settings and find Screen Time. Set daily limits for any app (fifteen minutes of TikTok, not three hours), schedule 'downtime' hours when only essential apps open, and lock the settings behind a passcode - ideally one held by a trusted person or one you genuinely do not remember. Limits sync across your Apple devices.

Start hereThe easiest first test on an iPhone: put a fifteen-minute daily limit on the two apps that steal your evenings, set the passcode, and see what the week feels like.

  • Why it is freeBuilt into every iPhone and iPad by Apple at no charge: nothing to download, no subscription, and no account beyond the Apple ID you already use. All of the useful features - app limits, downtime, the self-blocking passcode - are part of the free system.
  • Works oniPhone / iPad
  • Honest limitsiOS and iPadOS; the same Screen Time settings exist on Mac.

SelfControl

Start hereOpen source

A stubborn Mac blocker that keeps your time-wasting sites offline until the timer runs out, even if you delete the app first.

Pick the sites that derail you, set a timer for as many hours as you need, and click Start. SelfControl blocks those sites - and anything pulling from the same servers - until the timer ends. There is no pause button, no restart trick, and uninstalling does not turn it off early, which is exactly why it works when willpower runs out.

Start hereIf your focus battle happens on a Mac, this is the quickest honest test of whether blocking actually helps: pick three sites, try ninety minutes, and see what your day becomes.

  • Why it is freeThe source code is published openly for anyone to read and build, and the official site charges nothing for the app: no paid tier, no account, no license key. It is one independent developer's small, focused project offered to everyone for free.

Work quietly beside a person (on camera or on a stream) - the company of someone else working makes starting far easier.

Focusmate

Start hereFreeware

Book a real, live human to work beside you on camera, silently - three sessions a week free, forever.

Choose a twenty-five, fifty, or seventy-five minute slot, and Focusmate pairs you with another person somewhere in the world. Start hereThe first session feels strange for about five minutes and then never again - with three free sessions a week, the experiment costs nothing but honesty.

Browser calm-downs

Turn the web itself down: fewer ads and pop-ups, clean article views, and tab chaos quietly filed away.

Firefox Reader View

Start hereBuilt in

Firefox strips any article back to clean text at your font size, with nothing blinking, popping, or selling at you.

Tap the reader icon in the address bar (or press Ctrl+Alt+R) and the page collapses to a single readable column: no sidebars, no autoplay video beside your scroll, no cookie banners mid-article. Adjust the font, width, and background to suit your eyes, save articles into reading list, and even have Firefox read them aloud.

Start hereOne click to a calmer page - use it on the one article that defeated you this week, and attach the habit to that site from here on.

  • Why it is freeReader View is built into Firefox by Mozilla at no charge: no extension to trust, no account, no feature paywall - it is simply part of the browser.
  • Works onFirefox browser
  • Honest limitsFirefox on computers and Android; works best on article pages, not web apps.

uBlock Origin

Start hereOpen source

The proven open-source ad blocker: fewer pop-ups, fewer autoplaying videos, calmer pages - at no charge, with no premium tier at all.

uBlock Origin removes ads, pop-ups, autoplaying video, and tracking noise before they even load, which changes the emotional temperature of using the web: pages appear finished instead of twitching with things demanding clicks. It is highly configurable, but the default filter lists work well immediately; pause it per-site for any page that misbehaves.

Start hereIf you use Firefox, install it, leave the defaults alone, and enjoy the strange silence - the single biggest calm-down per unit of effort on this page.

Reader mode, but deeply customizable: your fonts, colors, and margins, saved as reusable themes for the sites you fight with most.

Tab groups (Chrome)

Easy winBuilt in

Color-coded, collapsible tab groups that turn a wall of forty tabs back into three quiet piles.

'Now', 'Wait', and 'Someday' groups give the browser a three-shelf filing cabinet, and collapsed groups stay collapsed across restarts when saved.
